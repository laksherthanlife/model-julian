# Yeast Digital Twin: Comprehensive Technical Writeup

Status: repository-grounded synthesis of completed computational experiments.
No new biological simulator, learner, Yeast9 dataset, or DBTL benchmark was run
for this document.

This report reconstructs the project from the current repository, canonical
validation narratives, executable scripts, and generated machine-readable
artifacts. It distinguishes completed evidence from proposed deployment ideas,
and it distinguishes physical-style simulated cultures from wet-lab cultures.

## 1. Executive Summary

The project asks whether a yeast digital twin can reduce the biological cost of
Design--Build--Test--Learn (DBTL) engineering. The intended workflow is:

```text
historical cultures
    -> learn a compact response model
    -> screen many candidate designs virtually
    -> verify a small candidate batch with exact mechanistic metabolism
    -> update the model with new observations
    -> repeat
```

The motivating asymmetry is practical. A culture, strain construction, or
sequential adaptive round is expensive; evaluating a candidate inside a frozen
computational model is comparatively cheap. The engineering objective is
therefore not simply low prediction error. It is whether computation can make a
fixed biological budget more effective, or reduce the number of sequential
biological stages needed to reach a useful verified design.

The project developed and audited a hybrid architecture combining a learned
compact state representation with exact genome-scale metabolism. The final
structural generator is deliberately different from the learner:

```text
external rxncon controls / genotype
    -> published Boolean rxncon cell-cycle regulator
    -> project-defined regulatory modules
    -> frozen 10-channel regulatory-to-GSM interface
    -> Yeast9 genome-scale LP and pFBA
    -> biomass, beta-carotene, fluxes, and biosensors
```

The regulatory ground truth is the published *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* CDC
rxncon model of Munzner, Klipp, and Krantz (Nature Communications, 2019,
doi:10.1038/s41467-019-08903-w). The metabolic ground truth is the repository's
Yeast9 asset, loaded through COBRApy and solved with the GLPK backend. The
regulatory-to-metabolic coupling, heterologous beta-carotene pathway, dynamic
objective protocol, and reporter model are project constructions. They are
mechanistically motivated but are not experimentally calibrated reconstructions
of real yeast regulation.

The central structural result is positive but bounded. A compact continuous
state-space learner approximated observable behavior generated by a much larger,
Boolean, published regulator coupled through the frozen interface to real
Yeast9/pFBA over a bounded externally controlled domain. This demonstrates a
reduced-order modeling result, not recovery of the true molecular state and not
validation of real yeast.

The central predictive result is more qualified. On the early real-GEM dataset,
direct environment-to-trajectory models were stronger than the state-space
models because beta-carotene trajectories were almost rank one. The active
rxncon benchmark preserved more regulatory variation, but product still
compressed it strongly. This is why the project does not claim universal
state-space superiority.

The final prospective results depend on which completed benchmark is being
described. The full powered prospective benchmark used 20 paired campaigns,
with 8 hybrid verification cultures versus 16 conventional cultures, and found
mean best final product `1.977756` versus `1.190680`; the paired difference was
`+0.787075`, bootstrap CI `[+0.083642,+1.565145]`, and the hybrid won 13 of 20
campaigns. That result is a raw-budget/stage-depth result, not an equal raw
culture-budget comparison. A later boundary-condition validation made the
common budget explicit: both methods received 8 prospective cultures per
campaign. Across 12 paired campaigns in four physiology worlds, hybrid mean
best final product was `1.731517` versus `0.812550` for conventional DBTL, a
mean difference of `+0.918967` with bootstrap CI `[+0.682314,+1.231545]` and
12/12 wins. The earlier 8-versus-16 result remains secondary context; the
common-8 result is the primary matched-budget endpoint for that later validation.

These results do not establish that hybrid DBTL universally beats conventional
DBTL. In the exact finite-pool acquisition benchmark, space filling ranked
first, while direct trajectory UCB and hybrid UCB were effectively tied. In the
strict final exact workflow audit, the hybrid did not show a robust reduction in
total cultures to fixed targets relative to conventional DBTL. The clearest
positive interpretation is narrower: under the tested synthetic prospective
protocol, extensive virtual search followed by a parallel verification batch
can reduce sequential biological stage depth and scenario calendar time while
retaining or improving verified quality.

Biosensors also produced a deliberately mixed result. The six reporters contain
real structured physiological information and are not direct copies of hidden
states. However, using them as training-time auxiliary targets worsened the
single beta-carotene prediction task after loss corrections. Genetic edits
increased hidden regulatory complexity but did not create a sufficiently strong
product-level edit task. A later hidden-history test stopped before Yeast9:
preconditioning differences disappeared from the frozen metabolic interface
under common post-transfer inputs. Live biosensor assimilation was therefore not
run. It must not be described as a failed live-sensor experiment; its generator
prerequisite was not present.

The strongest current conclusion is therefore:

> A compact learned model can approximate a bounded observable response
> manifold generated by a structurally different published yeast regulatory
> network coupled to exact Yeast9/pFBA, and a leakage-controlled twin workflow
> can improve prospective simulated DBTL stage efficiency in the tested worlds.
> The work does not establish real-yeast accuracy, universal hybrid superiority,
> universal biosensor usefulness, or live reporter state-assimilation benefit.

## 2. Problem Statement and Engineering Motivation

Classical DBTL is iterative:

```text
Design -> Build -> Test -> Learn -> Design -> ...
```

In metabolic engineering, each cycle can require strain construction, culture
growth, analytics, and interpretation. A search that is statistically efficient
in silico can still be experimentally inefficient if every candidate must be
built before it is evaluated. The digital-twin idea is to learn a response model
from historical cultures, screen a much larger candidate set computationally,
and reserve exact or physical measurements for a short list.

The asymmetry is not that computation is free. Exact Yeast9 simulations are
costly, and model training can be unstable. The relevant comparison is that
virtual evaluations can often be parallelized and reused, whereas sequential
biological feedback has a calendar and culture cost. A valid benchmark must
therefore count separately:

| Quantity | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Independent culture | One complete biological trajectory; the statistical unit |
| Timepoint | One observation within a culture; not an independent replicate |
| Simulation interval | One dynamic update between timepoints |
| LP solve | One growth, product, or pFBA optimization inside an interval |
| Virtual evaluation | A model-side candidate evaluation, not a culture |
| Exact verification culture | A candidate sent to the hidden exact simulator |
| Biological stage | A round whose proposals depend on previous exact observations |

The project repeatedly found that prediction error alone was insufficient. A
simple model can predict a compressed product curve very accurately without
representing the underlying regulation. Conversely, a mechanistic model can be
more auditable and more useful for intervention search without winning every
RMSE comparison. The final workflow therefore evaluates prediction, hidden-state
access, exact verification, culture accounting, and stage depth separately.

## 3. Conceptual Hybrid Architecture

The intended causal architecture is:

```text
environment / genotype / edit
             |
             v
     hidden regulation and physiology
             |
             v
      time-varying metabolic controls
             |
             v
          Yeast9 / pFBA
             |
             v
    biomass + product + observations
```

The learned component is a reduced-order state-space model. In its simplest
form, an environment and optional genotype initialize a compact latent state:

\[
z_0 = g_\theta(e,g),
\]

and the state evolves according to:

\[
z_{k+1} = z_k + \Delta t\,f_\theta(z_k,e,g).
\]

Intuitively, `z` is a compact memory of the physiological conditions that matter
for predicting future observables. It is not claimed to be a list of actual
proteins, Boolean rxncon states, or molecular concentrations. The Markov-style
assumption is that the future is approximately determined by the current compact
state and current controls rather than by the full raw history.

Depending on the experiment, decoder heads predict product, biomass, reporter
signals, or compact GSM-compatible controls. In the hybrid workflow, predicted
controls are passed to a fresh Yeast9/pFBA calculation; the learner does not
differentiate through Yeast9. Exact hidden verification uses the simulator, not
the learner's own predictions.

This separation creates an intentional model-class mismatch:

| Property | Ground-truth generator | Learned model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regulation | Large Boolean rxncon network | Compact continuous latent dynamics |
| Topology | Explicit reaction-contingency rules | Learned dense functions |
| Time update | Synchronous logical transitions | Smooth residual state update |
| Metabolism | Yeast9 LP/pFBA | Optional learned compact interface plus Yeast9 |
| Reporters | Delayed nonlinear mixed projections | Observable heads or online inputs |
| Hidden dimension | Thousands of compiled targets | Small validation-selected latent dimension |
| Objective | Exact growth/product/pFBA protocol | Prediction/design objective |

The mismatch is a feature. If the generator shared the learner's equations or
hidden coordinates, success would be less informative.

## 4. Development History

The project evolved through increasingly defensible tests rather than a single
architecture jump.

| Stage | Question | Result and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple ODE generators | Can a fixed environment produce learnable curves? | Yes, but the task was too easy and too close to the learner. |
| Hidden-state/reporters | Can auxiliary observations improve held-out prediction? | Sometimes, in deliberately designed toy worlds; not enough for biological claims. |
| Reduced stoichiometric surrogate | Does more mechanism make state-space learning necessary? | Direct models remained strong; the surrogate remained compressible. |
| Dynamic burden/state-machine models | Can stress, ATP, and bottlenecks create richer trajectories? | Mechanistic variation increased, but product shape remained low-dimensional. |
| Real Yeast9 pilot | Can exact genome-scale metabolism run dynamically? | Yes, with explicit LP accounting. |
| Dynamic pathway capacity | Can time-varying PSY/DES/CYC capacities enrich product dynamics? | The mechanism worked technically but did not remove rank-one product compression. |
| Early real-GEM model comparison | Does a state-space learner beat direct models? | No; polynomial/direct trajectory models were stronger. |
| Privileged-supervision audit | Were hidden controllers accidentally used as learner inputs? | Legacy oracle paths were separated from realistic observable-only benchmarks. |
| Published rxncon replacement | Can a structurally different regulator generate useful observable behavior? | Yes over a bounded active-input domain. |
| Reporter diagnostic | Do reporters help as auxiliary targets? | No robust product benefit; objective and task conflict were diagnosed. |
| Genetic-edit transfer | Do reporters help under unseen edits? | The edit/product task was weak; no reproducible reporter advantage. |
| Hidden-history screen | Can live reporters observe persistent unobserved history? | No: history collapsed before the frozen GSM interface. |

The important methodological change was a shift from “make the preferred model
win” to “identify which claim the current generator and task can support.”

## 5. Data Generator: Full Technical Description

This is the source of all synthetic labels. The final active-rxncon generator is:

\[
e,g
\longrightarrow r_t^{\mathrm{rxncon}}
\longrightarrow m_t
\longrightarrow c_t^{\mathrm{GSM}}
\longrightarrow \mathrm{Yeast9/pFBA}
\longrightarrow X(t),B(t),R(t).
\]

Here `r` is the hidden compiled Boolean regulatory state, `m` is a vector of
project-defined module activities, `c` is the ten-channel interface, `X` is
biomass, `B` is accumulated beta-carotene, and `R` contains biosensors. The
learner-facing tables contain only approved external inputs and observable
outputs. Raw states, modules, controls, constraints, fluxes, and simulator
regime labels are lockbox quantities.

### 5.1 Published rxncon regulatory model

The regulatory source is the published *S. cerevisiae* CDC model:

> Munzner, Klipp, and Krantz, “A comprehensive model of the yeast cell cycle,”
> Nature Communications (2019), doi:10.1038/s41467-019-08903-w.

The canonical workbook is:

```text
external_models/rxncon_screening/models/CDC_S_cerevisiae.xls
```

The repository records rxncon model provenance including model commit
`793c1407e36c64715c1e278fc03b2a3925fc28db` in the validation audit. The workbook
contains 804 reaction-list rows and 2729 contingency-list rows. The expanded
representation contains approximately 2664 rxncon reactions, 3720
contingencies, 1967 states, and 358 components. The compiled perturbable
Boolean network contains:

| Compiled object | Count |
| --- | ---: |
| Synchronous targets | 3874 |
| Reaction targets | 1095 |
| State targets | 2063 |
| KO targets | 358 |
| OE targets | 358 |
| Components | 358 |

Rxncon is a reaction-contingency formalism. A reaction is represented together
with conditions, contingencies, and state relationships that determine when it
can occur. The project compiled this representation into a deterministic
synchronous Boolean transition system:

\[
r_{t+1}=F_{\mathrm{rxncon}}(r_t,u_t).
\]

The compiled model evaluates all target updates from the previous synchronous
state, then applies the new state together. This is not an ODE and does not
pretend that one Boolean step is a calibrated biochemical time unit.

The structural external inputs used in the active-rxncon benchmark were:

```text
Nutrients, Pheromone, HU, LatA, Nocodazole
```

They represent nutrient availability, mating pheromone, hydroxyurea replication
stress, latrunculin-A cytoskeletal perturbation, and nocodazole spindle
perturbation. These are legitimate controls supported by the published model,
but they are structural-validation inputs, not a claim about the eventual iGEM
process control interface.

The compiler also supports KO/OE semantics. The later edit benchmark screened
these targets using the existing implementation; it did not invent arbitrary
latent perturbations. KO/OE edits were explicit learner inputs only in the edit
transfer experiment.

The original pyeda-based compilation path had a compatibility problem that could
segfault on the current runtime. The repository uses a narrow compatibility
repair that preserves the compiled reaction-contingency Boolean expressions and
synchronous update semantics while avoiding the unstable pyeda execution path.
The repair changes compilation/runtime compatibility, not the biological
topology or perturbation semantics. The resulting compiled network is audited
against the expected target and perturbation counts.

### 5.2 External-rxncon complexity screen

The rxncon screen asked whether the low dimensionality of product trajectories
was merely an artifact of the original hand-designed physiology. A broad,
regulation-only panel of approximately 1000 trajectories was generated over the
published external controls and KO/OE perturbations. Each trajectory had 49
aligned timepoints. Matched subsets of 125 complete trajectories were used for
uncertainty and comparison rather than treating the full panel as an unlimited
sample source.

The broad internal regulatory state had entropy effective rank approximately
40 and participation rank approximately 31. Simple phenotype/output summaries
collapsed much more strongly. This established the key distinction:

```text
nominal molecular/network complexity != externally accessible engineering complexity
```

For covariance eigenvalues `lambda_i`, the entropy effective rank is:

\[
D_{\mathrm{entropy}}
=\exp\left(-\sum_i p_i\ln p_i\right),
\qquad
p_i=\frac{\lambda_i}{\sum_j\lambda_j}.
\]

The participation ratio is:

\[
D_{\mathrm{PR}}
=\frac{(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2}{\sum_i\lambda_i^2}.
\]

These metrics describe the empirical variation in the visited trajectories. They
are not claims about the mathematical order of the rxncon system and are not
replacements for biological node counts.

### 5.3 Regulatory module aggregation

Thousands of Boolean targets cannot be passed directly into the metabolic
model. The project aggregates related states into module-level activity
summaries. A module is generally the mean activity of its mapped Boolean
states, producing a fractional value between zero and one at each timepoint.
The module definitions are project-defined and are not presented as an
additional published model. They summarize broad programs such as:

- G1/start CDK activity;
- S-phase and replication;
- G2/M cyclins;
- APC exit/separase activity;
- DNA-damage/replication checkpoint;
- spindle checkpoint;
- morphogenesis/spindle/actin;
- mating/polarity and related regulatory programs.

The module layer is an interpretive interface. It preserves multiple distinct
rxncon directions, but it is already an information reduction. Any smoothing or
persistence in the module-to-metabolism path is a project-side biochemical-time
abstraction, not a published rxncon rule.

### 5.4 The ten-channel rxncon-to-GSM interface

The frozen v1 interface maps module activities and direct environment inputs to
time-varying metabolic controls. It is deterministic, bounded, smoothed, and
fixed before learner evaluation. The table below separates the intended
mechanism from its evidence status.

| Channel | Yeast9 target or role | Main contributors | Mapping character | Scientific status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `carbon_uptake_capacity` | Glucose exchange lower bound `r_1714` | Nutrients, glucose uptake, checkpoint and mating modules | Direct environment multiplied by higher-level attenuation | Plausible abstraction; not calibrated |
| `oxygen_capacity` | Oxygen exchange lower bound `r_1992` | DO, DNA checkpoint, spindle checkpoint | DO-scaled respiratory capacity with stress attenuation | Plausible abstraction; not calibrated |
| `atp_maintenance_multiplier` | ATP maintenance `r_4046` | HU, LatA, nocodazole, checkpoint and morphogenesis modules | Additive bounded maintenance demand | Synthetic but directionally defensible |
| `growth_allocation_gamma` | Preserved biomass-growth fraction | G1, S, G2/M, checkpoint and mating modules | Growth support minus arrest/diversion terms | Synthetic higher-level allocation rule |
| `stress_maintenance_load` | Maintenance and beta-carotene loss burden | External stresses, checkpoints, mating | Bounded additive stress burden | Synthetic production-burden rule |
| `precursor_availability` | Native GGPP `r_0461` effective bound | Growth and biosynthetic modules, checkpoint stress | Bounded resource/precursor allocation | Plausible abstraction; not GPR-grounded |
| `resource_translation_capacity` | Global heterologous enzyme/resource allocation | Growth, replication, APC, checkpoint and mating modules | Bounded many-to-many resource control | Synthetic higher-level rule |
| `PSY_capacity` | `BETA_PHYTOENE_SYNTHASE` bound | Growth/cell-cycle modules, DNA checkpoint | Resource-sensitive heterologous capacity | Synthetic pathway control |
| `DES_capacity` | `BETA_PHYTOENE_DESATURASE` bound | S/G2/APC modules, DNA and spindle checkpoints | Stress-sensitive downstream capacity | Synthetic pathway control |
| `CYC_capacity` | `BETA_LYCOPENE_CYCLASE` bound | G2/APC/morphogenesis modules, checkpoints | Stress-sensitive terminal capacity | Synthetic pathway control |

Conceptually, for reaction or capacity target `j`:

\[
v_j^{\max}(t)=a_j(t)V_j^{\max},
\]

where `a_j(t)` is a bounded function of several module values and, where
appropriate, direct environmental values. Each channel is recursively smoothed
with a fixed project parameter so that a Boolean change does not create an
instantaneous infinite biochemical response. The exact coefficients, clipping
limits, and smoothing constants are implemented in
`src/yeast_validation/run_rxncon_gsm_generator_gate.py`.

An overlap audit identified approximately 127 rxncon/Yeast9 label-match rows.
That is useful evidence of biological vocabulary overlap, but the active
interface is not predominantly direct GPR regulation. Most channels are
higher-level mappings from module activity to exchange, maintenance,
allocation, precursor, or heterologous pathway capacity. The interface is
therefore suitable for a structural synthetic test, not a quantitatively
validated reconstruction of yeast regulatory control of Yeast9 reactions.

### 5.5 Yeast9 genome-scale metabolism

The canonical Yeast9 checksum is:

```text
9fd2c572cace73c2ea835205617313554d1bf89f4ef077f49defbdfa219a4ad7
```

COBRApy loads the asset as `yeastGEM_v9__46__0__46__2`. The model contains:

| Quantity | Count or identifier |
| --- | --- |
| Reactions | 4131 |
| Metabolites | 2806 |
| Genes | 1161 |
| Biomass objective | `r_2111` |
| Glucose exchange | `r_1714` |
| Oxygen exchange | `r_1992` |
| ATP maintenance | `r_4046` |
| Native GGPP reaction | `r_0461` |

The solver path is COBRApy/optlang with GLPK (`swiglpk`) in the recorded
environment. A GEM contains thousands of nominal flux variables, but the
complexity audits use empirical variation across visited flux states rather
than reaction count.

The distinction between regulation and metabolism is fundamental. Rxncon
updates a Boolean regulatory state. Yeast9 then solves a constrained linear
program under stoichiometric mass balance and bounds. Yeast9 is not
differentiated through in the primary experiments.

### 5.6 Heterologous beta-carotene pathway

Beta-carotene is the engineering product used throughout the later exact
benchmarks. The loaded GEM is copied and augmented with:

```text
BETA_PHYTOENE_SYNTHASE
BETA_PHYTOENE_DESATURASE
BETA_LYCOPENE_CYCLASE
DM_beta_carotene_c
```

Native GGPP formation through `r_0461` is reused. The three heterologous steps
drain GGPP through a lumped phytoene/lycopene sequence into an accumulation
demand for beta-carotene. The demand sink is intentionally non-balanced: it is a
synthetic product-accumulation objective rather than a complete biochemical
reconstruction with export, degradation, and cofactor accounting for every
step. Disabling PSY, DES, or CYC eliminates product flux in the pathway
continuity audit.

### 5.7 Dynamic Yeast9/pFBA simulation

Each dynamic interval uses the current regulatory/metabolic controls to build a
fresh constrained model and performs staged optimization:

1. maximize growth;
2. preserve a required state-dependent fraction of growth;
3. maximize beta-carotene under the preserved growth allocation;
4. preserve the product solution;
5. run deterministic parsimonious FBA;
6. record fluxes, constraints, solver status, and accounting;
7. update biomass and accumulated product;
8. carry state-dependent burdens and capacities into the next interval.

With timestep `Delta t`, the core dynamic updates are conceptually:

\[
X_{t+1}=X_t+\Delta t\,v_{\mathrm{growth},t}X_t,
\]

\[
B_{t+1}=\max\left(0,B_t+\Delta t\,[v_{\beta,t}X_t-k_{\mathrm{loss},t}B_t]\right).
\]

The exact interval protocol uses three LP categories per interval: growth,
product, and pFBA. Thus a 49-timepoint culture with 48 intervals has 144 LP
optimizations in the dense exact protocol. A “culture” is not one LP, and a
timepoint is not one culture. The solver ledger records growth/product/pFBA
counts, optimal/infeasible/unbounded counts, skipped intervals, and surrogate
evaluations separately.

### 5.8 Dynamic burden and capacity states

Before rxncon became the regulatory ground truth, the project explored explicit
dynamic engineering states including oxidative burden, ATP pressure,
bottleneck/congestion, ER diagnostics, and pathway capacities `E_PSY`, `E_DES`,
and `E_CYC`. Fluxes and product burden could update these states, and the states
could change later GEM bounds. This layer was useful for engineering the exact
GEM protocol, but it must not be conflated with the published rxncon network.

The selected capacity pilot used bounds in `[0.08,1]`, synthesis rates
`0.084/0.072/0.066`, baseline decay rates `0.018/0.024/0.022`, and selected
congestion sensitivities `0.320` and `0.280`. It altered exact pathway
capacities and passed solver accounting, but product trajectories remained
nearly rank one. It was therefore a technically successful but task-complexity
negative result.

### 5.9 Biosensor generation

The active generator emits six biosensors:

```text
R_stress
R_resource
R_checkpoint
R_pathway_capacity
R_morphology
R_energy
```

They are intended as partial experimental measurements, not direct state
exports. Each reporter is a delayed, filtered, nonlinear mixture of several
module/interface/environment contributors:

\[
R_k(t)=h_k\left(\sum_i w_{ki}q_i(t-\tau_{ki})
 +\sum_{i,j}w_{kij}q_i(t-\tau_i)q_j(t-\tau_j)\right)+\epsilon_k(t).
\]

The implementation applies contributor weights, finite lag, sigmoid saturation,
first-order filtering, culture-level gain/baseline variation, and modest
measurement noise. Clean/noiseless reporters are retained for simulator
diagnostics; noisy reporters are the learner-facing measurements.

Reporter construction safeguards were audited on the active rxncon dataset:

| Audit | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Maximum reporter/raw-hidden correlation | approximately 0.678 |
| Maximum reporter/reporter correlation | approximately 0.633 |
| Mean hidden-PC reconstruction R2 | approximately 0.262 |

The reporter is therefore informative but non-invertible. This matters because a
reporter equal to one hidden simulator state would turn a difficult state
estimation problem into privileged label exposure.

inputs.

### 5.12 Sparse/event-triggered Yeast9

Dense exact simulation performs an LP protocol at every dynamic interval. The
active-rxncon generator introduced event-triggered replay: solve when the
interface changes beyond a normalized threshold, at the first and final steps,
and after a maximum allowed gap; otherwise carry forward the last exact flux and
constraint solution.

The sparse path was validated against dense replay on diverse cultures before
the full active-rxncon run. The active dataset contains 125 cultures, 49
timepoints, and 5,043 actual LP solves. The dense/sparse validation showed
approximately 70.8% median LP reduction, with final-product differences on the
order of a few times `10^-6` and negligible biomass/product RMSE relative to
between-culture variation. Sparse replay therefore changes compute accounting,
not the accepted phenotype definition. It does not authorize replacing Yeast9
with a surrogate.

### 5.13 Generator complexity ladder

The active rxncon dataset gives the most useful information-compression ladder:

| Layer | Nominal interpretation | Entropy effective rank | Participation rank |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Raw rxncon state | Thousands of compiled Boolean targets | 5.45 | approximately 4--5 |
| Regulatory modules | Project-defined physiological summaries | 4.55 | approximately 3--4 |
| GSM interface | Ten time-varying controls | 5.22 | approximately 4--5 |
| Yeast9 metabolic state | Selected empirical flux state | 2.91 | approximately 2--3 |
| Noiseless biosensors | Structured biological reporter signals | 3.35 | approximately 2--3 |
| Biological observables | Biomass, product, reporters | 4.10 | approximately 3--4 |
| Product trajectory | Beta-carotene alone | 1.27 | approximately 1 |

The broad KO/OE rxncon screen reached approximately 40 internal entropy rank,
but the environment-only active task reached approximately 5.45. That is not a
contradiction. The first is organism complexity under broad intervention; the
second is task complexity conditional on the allowed external controls.

The ladder shows where variation is lost. Much of the environment-accessible
regulatory variation survives module and interface aggregation. Yeast9 metabolic
states compress it to roughly three empirical modes, and the chosen product
projection compresses it further to approximately one dominant mode. This is
why a direct product model can be extremely strong without reconstructing
regulatory biology.

### 5.14 Gene-edit generator complexity

The compiler-supported edit screen examined 155 perturbations: 75 KO and 80 OE.
No KO target propagated into the selected interface; 29 OE targets propagated,
and 18 diverse OE targets were selected for the canonical transfer dataset.
The selection used generator-side propagation and diversity, not learner
performance.

The edit-transfer dataset contains 200 complete cultures and 8,418 exact Yeast9
LP solves. Its empirical ranks were:

| Layer | Entropy rank |
| --- | ---: |
| rxncon | 17.79 |
| Modules | 7.80 |
| GSM interface | 5.69 |
| Yeast9 metabolic flux | 2.79 |
| Biosensors | 3.80 |
| Biological observables | 4.73 |
| Product | 1.60 |

Edits substantially increased hidden regulatory complexity, but the beta-

### 5.15 Generator defensibility and limitations

The generator contains different levels of biological grounding:

| Component | Source | Published or engineered? | Experimentally validated? | Role | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| rxncon network | 2019 published CDC model | Published biology | Not validated here against new wet-lab data | Hidden regulation | Cell-cycle-focused, not complete yeast regulation |
| Boolean compiler | Repository compatibility path | Engineering implementation | Count/provenance audited | Executable regulation | Boolean time is not calibrated biochemical time |
| External rxncon controls | Published model inputs | Published interface semantics | No new wet-lab calibration | Structural perturbations | Not final industrial process variables |
| Module aggregation | Project definitions | Engineered | No | Reduce state for coupling | Hand-defined module boundaries |
| Ten-channel interface | Project mapping | Engineered, biologically motivated | No quantitative calibration | Regulation-to-metabolism bridge | Mostly not direct GPR regulation |

mechanistically constrained synthetic ground truth with real stoichiometric
metabolism, explicit solver accounting, and a learner that cannot see its
regulatory topology. It can test whether a reduced-order workflow handles model
class mismatch and exact verification. It cannot establish real-yeast biology.

## 6. Learner Architecture

The primary learner family is a compact continuous state-space model. It maps
approved external inputs to a latent initial state and rolls the state forward:

\[
z_0=g_\theta(e,g),
\qquad
z_{t+1}=z_t+\Delta t f_\theta(z_t,e,g).
\]

Decoder heads can predict product, biomass, reporter trajectories, or compact
GSM-compatible controls and flux summaries in teacher/student diagnostics.

The principal realistic learner does not receive raw rxncon states, module
truth, true GSM controls, constraints, or fluxes. Hidden truth can be compared
post hoc after the model is frozen, but it is not a training objective,
checkpoint criterion, or deployment input.

Three model concepts must be distinguished:

1. **Direct product model.** Predicts product or trajectory coefficients from
   environment/genotype directly.
2. **State-space model.** Learns a compact latent dynamics and observable heads.
3. **Hybrid model.** Predicts a compact interface, then calls exact Yeast9/pFBA
   to produce or verify product and biomass.

Some early teacher/student experiments used differentiable or reduced controls
as training aids. These are not the same as the realistic learner. The most
important deployment claim uses observable inputs and exact hidden verification.

## 7. Baselines

The project used strong baselines because nominal biological complexity does not
justify a complex learner if the selected output is simple:

- mean trajectory;
- nearest-environment or nearest-edit;
- linear PCA coefficient regression;
- polynomial environment-to-PCA regression;
- multi-output MLP;
- coordinate-conditioned MLP;
- tree/boosted baselines where appropriate;
- direct trajectory UCB and black-box BO in design benchmarks;
- space-filling and random search.

The baselines test whether the hybrid architecture adds predictive or
engineering value beyond learning the observed response manifold directly.

## 8. Early Real-GEM / Low-Dimensional Result

The first large exact GEM diagnostic used 125 cultures from a `5 x 5 x 5`
temperature/pH/DO grid, with 49 timepoints and 48 intervals. It completed
18,000 exact LP solves: 6,000 growth, 6,000 product, and 6,000 pFBA. The
culture-level split was 77 train, 15 validation, 15 interpolation, 10
held-out-combination, and 8 extrapolation cultures.

On held-out combinations, trajectory-normalized RMSE was:

| Model | RMSE |
| --- | ---: |
| Polynomial environment-to-PCA | 0.002492 |
| Multi-output MLP | 0.005622 |
| Coordinate-conditioned MLP | 0.008639 |
| Mechanistic-rate state-space | 0.012561 |
| Product-only state-space | 0.023712 |
| ATP reporter state-space | 0.020308 |
| Oxidative reporter state-space | 0.020457 |
| Combined reporter state-space | 0.029127 |
| Oracle state/capacity supervision | 0.028356 |

The product trajectory's first principal component explained approximately
99.8% of its variation. The direct model learned environment-to-curve
coefficients without reconstructing hidden physiology. The paired best

with zero state-space wins and bootstrap CI `[+0.007374,+0.011401]`.

This was not simply a neural failure. It demonstrated that task dimensionality,
not nominal simulator size, determines whether mechanistic inductive bias is
necessary for the selected output.

### 8.1 Current-system complexity baseline

The cached current-system audit provides a complementary baseline before the

| Layer | Entropy rank | Participation rank | Main interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Raw product | 1.017 | 1.005 | Essentially rank one |
| Normalized product | 1.018 | 1.005 | Amplitude is not the source of complexity |
| Hidden physiology | 2.789 | 2.406 | Compact latent simulator state |

Product PC1 explained `99.7626%` of raw product variance and `99.7750%` after

This audit is why the later active-rxncon result is interpreted as a task

## 9. Privileged Supervision and Observability Repairs

Several early branches used hidden controller or pathway-capacity values because
they were convenient training labels. This can produce a convincing result for
the wrong reason: a real experiment cannot measure exact simulator capacities,
future fluxes, or hidden bottleneck identity.

The project therefore separated:

- realistic learner-facing environment, genotype, biomass, product, and reporter
  quantities;
- oracle/teacher diagnostics that explicitly label hidden truth;
- post hoc latent alignment performed only after model freeze.

This repair also changed how success was described. A learned latent coordinate
is not evidence of a recovered biological variable unless an independent
measurement validates it. The primary target became accurate observable behavior
and leakage-free design decisions.

## 10. Published rxncon Active Structural Validation

### 10.1 Question and design

The active structural validation asked:

> Can a compact continuous model approximate observable behavior generated by a
> much larger, mechanistically different published Boolean regulator coupled
> through a frozen interface to real Yeast9?

All 32 static combinations of the five supported rxncon inputs were audited,
with additional temporal schedules. The regulation-only screen found 52
distinct active rxncon trajectories and 32 distinct final states. The canonical
coupled dataset contained 125 complete cultures with 49 timepoints, culture-level
splits, six mixed biosensors, and 5,043 sparse exact LP solves.

### 10.2 Complexity and learning result

The active coupled complexity ladder was the one reported in Section 5.13.
The no-reporter state-space model had mean non-train product NRMSE approximately
`0.204` in the completed active structural benchmark. The corresponding
training-time real-reporter condition was approximately `0.857`, while shuffled
and random auxiliary controls were approximately `0.761` and `0.518` in the
initial diagnostic. The result was not interpreted as “reporters are bad” in
general; it showed that auxiliary reporter reconstruction can be harmful to a
single compressed product objective.

The bounded positive structural claim is:

> A compact continuous state-space learner can approximate the task-relevant
> observable response of a larger published Boolean regulatory network coupled
> to real Yeast9/pFBA over the tested active external-input domain.

The claim does not include recovery of the 3874 Boolean targets, exact latent
coordinate correspondence, or complete yeast regulation.

## 11. Reporter and Biosensor Investigation

### 11.1 Why reporters were considered

Early toy generators showed that a reporter can help when it measures hidden
state that product alone does not identify. This motivated the idea that
biosensors might ground a learned physiological representation and make it more
useful for intervention or online state estimation.

### 11.2 Training-time reporter supervision

In the active rxncon diagnostic, the original objective was effectively:

\[
L=L_{\mathrm{product}}+L_{\mathrm{biomass}}
 +\sum_k L_{R_k}.
\]

At initialization, the unnormalized gradients contributed approximately 10%
from product, 13% from biomass, and 77% from the reporter terms. The original
summed reporter objective produced product NRMSE approximately `0.475`, versus
approximately `0.221` for product-only in the diagnostic. Per-channel
training-only normalization and averaging reduced the degradation but did not
create a robust positive lambda.

The product/reporter shared-gradient cosine was approximately `0.013` on
average: reporter gradients were mostly orthogonal to product gradients, not
strongly anti-aligned. This supports a negative multitask-transfer
interpretation rather than a simple catastrophic sign conflict.

The effective dimensions explain the result: product was approximately 1.27
dimensional, biosensors approximately 3.35, and raw environment-accessible
rxncon approximately 5.45. A small latent model can predict the narrow product
projection while reconstructing six reporters asks it to preserve additional
physiology that is not needed for beta-carotene.

The diagnostic classified the failure using evidence for reporter overweighting,
loss scaling, insufficient latent capacity, and reporter information that was
not product-relevant. It did not establish that the reporters lack physiological
information.

### 11.3 Channel-specific diagnostics

`R_energy` was the strongest individual product-relevant reporter after
conditioning on controls. `R_morphology` carried substantial regulatory
variation but was less directly related to product. This is biologically
interpretable: a reporter can be an excellent marker of a real physiological
program while being irrelevant or distracting for one production objective.

### 11.4 Genetic-edit transfer hypothesis

The next hypothesis was that reporters might help when unseen genetic edits
activate physiological modes that fixed-strain product prediction can ignore.
The frozen generator was tested over 18 selected OE targets and 200 cultures.
The hidden rxncon rank increased to 17.79, but product rank was still only 1.60.
Training-time reporter supervision did not produce a reproducible advantage on
unseen single edits, unseen combinations, or the hard edit-family split. Strong
direct baselines remained competitive or better.

### 11.5 Edit-effect identifiability gate

Before testing live edited-culture reporter assimilation, the project audited
whether genotype actually mattered to the product target. Final-product variance
decomposition gave:

| Predictor | R2 |
| --- | ---: |
| Environment only | 0.563 |
| Genotype only | 0.127 |
| Environment plus genotype | 0.603 |
| Incremental genotype given environment | 0.040 |

The median edit trajectory effect was approximately `0.0004198`, only about
`0.144` times the reference-strain environmental RMS variation. PCA ridge gave
mean NRMSE approximately `0.163` with environment plus genotype, `0.168` with
environment only, `0.168` with the reference genotype vector, and `0.235` with
shuffled genotype on the main transfer splits. The machine-readable decision
was `EDIT_EFFECT_MEANINGFUL=False`.

This means the intended unseen-edit product task was too weak: apparent transfer
performance was dominated by environmental prediction. The live edit-assimilation
experiment was not run.

### 11.6 Hidden-history test

The strongest remaining biosensor hypothesis was that live reporters could
measure an unobserved physiological history in otherwise identical cultures.
The design required:

```text
same genotype + same production environment + same initial X/B
different hidden preconditioning history
    -> different persistent rxncon/interface state
    -> different future product
```

The cheap screen evaluated 50 predeclared histories using nutrient withdrawal,
pheromone, HU, LatA, nocodazole, pulse durations, recovery intervals, and
sequential controls. Transfer-state entropy rank was 4.72 and participation
rank 4.44, so the preconditioning operation did create some transfer-state
variation.

However, under the common post-transfer input clamp, production rxncon,
regulatory-module, and GSM-interface ranks were each approximately 1.0. Only
HU retained an internal `[dNTP]` difference; the frozen interface had no `[dNTP]`
channel. Longer 6--200-step pulses did not create persistent interface memory.

The history/interface gate failed before Yeast9. No pilot cultures, LP solves,
or assimilation learners were run. This is a generator-side negative result,
not a negative live-biosensor result. The missing prerequisite was persistent
history reaching the metabolic interface.

### 11.7 Current biosensor interpretation

The evidence supports a task-dependent design principle rather than an
automatic reporter claim. Training-time auxiliary supervision forces a compact
model to explain all reporter variation. That can hurt a compressed product
objective. A future online system should instead treat sensors as candidate
observations:

```text
environment + genotype + early product/biomass + selected reporters
    -> state estimate
    -> future product / intervention prediction
```

This is a proposed deployment workflow, not a completed live-assimilation
result. Because retraining is cheap relative to wet-lab work, a practical system
can validate no-sensor, individual-sensor, and sensor-subset models and reject
measurements that do not improve held-out performance. This does not remove the
need for culture-level validation or uncertainty calibration.

The final data-sufficiency experiment subsampled complete training trajectories

## 13. Prospective DBTL Benchmark

### 13.1 Full powered prospective benchmark

The full powered benchmark used 10 campaign seeds in each of two hidden
physiology worlds: `baseline_world` and `strong_oxidative_burden_world`.
Conventional DBTL used four online batches of four cultures (`4+4+4+4`); the
hybrid performed 6,000 virtual evaluations and one exact verification batch of
eight cultures.

Aggregate accounting was:

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Campaign pairs | 20/20 |
| Conventional cultures | 320 |
| Hybrid verification cultures | 160 |
| Yeast9 LP solves | 69,120 |

| Reused valid prospective sidecars | 212 |
| Solver failures/infeasible/unbounded/skipped | 0 |
| Old finite-pool cache access | 0 |

Mean best final product was `1.190680` for conventional DBTL and `1.977756`

`[+0.083642,+1.565145]`, with 13 hybrid wins and 7 losses. Mean best

hybrid used 8 verification cultures and one verification stage; conventional
used 16 cultures and four stages. It is valid evidence for the tested

World-specific means were also positive, but only three paired runs per world

The secondary reused baseline/oxidative context retained the raw 8-versus-16

### 13.3 Accounting and leakage

The benchmark separates exact cultures, virtual evaluations, LP solves, and
biological stages. Hybrid virtual search did not receive exact future outcomes.
Conventional later proposals were timestamped after prior exact outcomes. Old
finite-pool acquisition caches were not used as hidden observations. Exact rows
used the Yeast9 LP backend and recorded solver status. The local/Vanda smoke
test preserved final products and candidate ordering within numerical tolerance.

The result is therefore a workflow claim under a synthetic hidden-wet-lab
protocol, not a claim that a neural model's own predictions are accurate enough
to replace exact verification.

### 13.4 Strict multi-workflow exact audit and wall-clock interpretation

The repository also contains a larger strict exact workflow audit with
`13,920/13,920` valid hidden-wet-lab tasks. This is a separate endpoint from the
20-paired-campaign prospective benchmark above. It compares conventional DBTL,
Bayesian optimization, a black-box twin, a modular hybrid without reporters,
and a reporter-informed modular hybrid under a fixed operational scenario. It
is useful because it charges requested cultures and separates target attainment,
total cultures, biological stages, calendar time, and best verified quality.

The strict moderate-target result was:

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Conventional DBTL | 0.533333 | 20.666667 | 3.200000 | 31.000000 | 1.828228 |
| Bayesian optimization | 0.166667 | 22.933333 | 3.766667 | 36.666667 | 1.506834 |
| Black-box twin | 0.466667 | 23.466667 | 1.966667 | 17.700000 | 1.864079 |

## 14. Boundary Conditions and Robustness

The prospective result is conditional on the training data, candidate universe,
hidden worlds, objective, and accounting protocol. The data-sufficiency sweep
tested trajectory-level culture counts down to 10. The physiology robustness
validation added ATP-limited and pathway-bottleneck worlds without changing the
learner architecture or treating hidden world labels as inputs. The common-8
advantage remained positive in all four tested worlds, but the number of campaign
seeds was small in the added worlds.

The project also found an important counterexample. In the exact finite-pool
benchmark, 96 candidates across two regimes required 13,824 LP solves. The
methods were compared under an equal hidden-query acquisition protocol. Mean
exact-pool regret ranked:

| Rank | Method | Mean exact-pool regret |
| ---: | --- | ---: |
| 1 | Space-filling parallel | 0.265236 |
| 2 | Direct trajectory UCB | 0.303118 |
| 3 | Hybrid digital-twin UCB | 0.303241 |
| 4 | Black-box BO | 0.344624 |
| 5 | Random parallel | 0.421701 |
| 6 | Static GEM | 0.499934 |

This does not contradict the prospective stage-depth result. The benchmarks
ask different questions:

| Benchmark | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Finite pool | Which method uses an equal exact-query budget best? |
| Prospective workflow | Can virtual evaluations replace sequential biological stages before a small exact batch? |

The hybrid has no clear acquisition advantage in the first question. Its
positive result is in the second, under the tested candidate search and parallel
verification interpretation.

## 15. Open-Ended Deployment Tests

The repository also contains deployment-style studies in which a public-facing
agent or scientist-like workflow receives public model/reaction information and
submits candidates to a hidden exact verifier. These studies enforce candidate
validity, reaction-edit universes, exact solver accounting, and method-specific
observation access. The public-bundle agent is not a human scientist, and mixed
open-ended outcomes are not evidence of universal hybrid superiority.

The public editable universe contains 240 native Yeast9 reaction IDs across 39
subsystems plus a curated heterologous library. Public static planning used one
FBA solve, one pFBA solve, and 480 FVA LP solves. The public bundle excludes the
hidden regulator, exact dynamic outcomes, hybrid checkpoints, successful strain
lists, and narrative outcome summaries.

In the first open-ended campaign, the public scientist's best verified final
product was `0.571041` and the hybrid's was `0.559266`; the hybrid was
non-inferior under the predeclared 0.05 margin but not superior. Across the
three valid replicated campaigns, the scientist won best verified objective in
all three campaigns, while the hybrid won mean verified objective in all three.
Hybrid best-objective non-inferiority held in only one of three campaigns. The
correct classification is mixed and campaign-dependent, not a universal
open-ended hybrid win.

The readiness audit found that many syntactically valid sparse edits had no
verified influence on the hidden trajectory. Campaign 002 contained eight
requested hybrid candidates but only one effective phenotype and one objective
value. This was classified as effective intervention collapse, not cache or
hash corruption. The open-ended benchmark therefore remains useful as a
protocol and readiness audit, but it is not the strongest performance evidence
in this report.

The current practical evidence is strongest for the bounded prospective
benchmark, not for claims that a fully autonomous agent has solved open-ended
yeast engineering.

## 16. Why Direct Models Often Match the Hybrid

The central conceptual distinction is:

\[
D_{\mathrm{task}}
\neq
\text{number of molecular variables}.
\]

A 3874-target hidden regulatory network can produce a response manifold with
effective rank around 5 under one allowed intervention domain. Yeast9 fluxes can
then have rank around 3 and beta-carotene trajectories rank around 1.3. A direct
model can learn:

```text
environment / genotype -> product trajectory manifold
```

without learning the hidden physiology. That is not evidence that regulation is
simple. It is evidence that the selected output loses information.

This finding explains three otherwise confusing results:

1. direct polynomial/PCA models beat state-space models on the early real-GEM
   prediction task;
2. hidden rxncon complexity increased substantially under edits while product
   stayed compressed;
3. space filling can beat an elaborate hybrid in a finite exact candidate pool.

Mechanistic inductive bias is most valuable when the intervention or observation
task exposes modes that the direct output does not collapse away.

## 17. What the State-Space/Hybrid Model Contributes

The project does not support “state-space always predicts better.” The supported
contributions are different:

- a compact representation of task-relevant dynamics;
- a clean interface between learned regulation and exact metabolism;
- explicit separation of learner information from simulator truth;
- mechanistic verification of virtual candidates;
- a place to incorporate heterogeneous observations;
- a potential basis for future online state estimation;
- workflow accounting that distinguishes virtual search from biological cost.

Exact oracle replay showed that the compact interface can reproduce saved GEM
dynamics when the true controls are supplied, with one-culture oracle replay
product RMSE `2.61e-16` in the completed pilot. That is an interface/replay
validation, not proof that the learned interface is biologically correct.

The final positive workflow result shows that virtual search plus parallel exact
verification can be operationally useful in the tested worlds. The finite-pool
negative result shows that modularity itself does not guarantee better
acquisition.

## 18. Current Practical Digital-Twin Workflow

The most defensible engineering workflow is:

```text
historical environment/genotype/product/biomass/reporter data
    -> culture-level train/validation/test split
    -> train several candidate representations
    -> select by held-out predictive and calibration criteria
    -> optionally predict compact GSM-compatible controls
    -> exact Yeast9/pFBA verification of shortlisted designs
    -> parallel physical or exact culture batch
    -> add observations and repeat
```

The sensor contract should remain empirical. Candidate models can include no
sensors, individual sensors, biologically grouped sensors, or all sensors. A
model that depends on a reporter must demonstrate value on held-out cultures and
the relevant intervention split. The training-time reporter-negative result
means that reporters should not be forced into a shared loss merely because
they exist.

The workflow does not eliminate overfitting. Complete cultures remain the
independent units, validation must be trajectory-level, hidden exact verification
must be separate from learner scoring, and candidate search must be audited for
future-outcome leakage.

## 19. Trial and Error: Lessons Learned

| Problem | What was tried | What happened | Lesson / change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simple ODE was too easy | More nonlinear curves and hidden states | Direct models remained strong | Test task complexity, not visual complexity |
| Reduced surrogate was too low-dimensional | Faster burden and state-machine mechanisms | Product remained compressible | Do not equate mechanism count with task rank |
| Exact GEM was expensive | Event-triggered replay | Large LP reduction with negligible replay error | Sparse exact compute is acceptable after validation |
| Dynamic capacities were weak | PSY/DES/CYC congestion states | Mechanism changed but product rank stayed high-compression | Capacity dynamics alone do not guarantee state-space need |
| Privileged controller labels | Teacher/oracle supervision | Useful diagnostics but not deployable evidence | Keep oracle paths separate |
| Reporter loss dominated | Normalize channels and sweep weights | Penalty reduced but did not become a robust gain | Reporter reconstruction can be negative multitask transfer |
| Reporters were orthogonal to product | Gradient and channel diagnostics | Physiology information did not imply product information | Treat sensors as task-dependent observations |
| Genetic edits increased hidden rank | 18-edit transfer dataset | Product effects remained weak | Audit edit identifiability before sensor transfer |
| History memory was proposed | 50 rxncon preconditioning histories | Difference disappeared before GSM | Do not call a blocked downstream test negative |
| Finite-pool hybrid was expected to win | Exact equal-query benchmark | Space filling ranked first | Separate acquisition efficiency from stage reduction |
| Queue/runtime risk | Vanda process-per-culture workers | Exact workloads completed with solver accounting | Parallelize independent cultures while controlling memory |

## 20. Claims Supported Now

### Strongest structural claim

A compact continuous model can approximate bounded observable trajectories
generated by a substantially larger, structurally different published yeast CDC
rxncon network coupled through a fixed engineered interface to real Yeast9/pFBA.

### Engineering DBTL claim

In the completed synthetic prospective benchmark, a pretrained twin used many
virtual evaluations and a small parallel exact verification batch to obtain
higher mean best verified product than the tested sequential conventional
workflow. In the later matched-8 validation, this advantage persisted across
four tested synthetic physiology worlds. The result is computational and
prospective within the hidden simulator.

### Biosensor claim

The six reporters contain structured physiological information, but forcing a
compact model to reconstruct all reporters during training did not improve
single-product beta-carotene prediction. The edit-transfer and hidden-history
tests did not establish a live-sensor advantage. Sensor value remains
task-dependent and should be tested as an observation/assimilation question.

## 21. Claims Not Supported

The project does not establish:

- predictive accuracy on real yeast cultures;
- real wet-lab DBTL acceleration;
- universal state-space or hybrid superiority;
- universal biosensor usefulness;
- live reporter state-assimilation benefit;
- unique recovery of true cellular regulatory states;
- a quantitatively validated rxncon-to-GSM regulatory reconstruction;
- genome-wide genetic-intervention generalization;
- globally optimal strain designs;
- that 10 or 50 real cultures are universally sufficient;
- that rxncon represents all yeast regulation;
- that the tested rxncon perturbations reproduce an industrial process.

## 22. Limitations and Future Work

The next scientific boundary is real experimental data. Important priorities
are:

1. collect complete culture trajectories with biological replicates and measured
   process variables;
2. calibrate GPR-grounded regulatory control where rxncon components and Yeast9
   genes genuinely overlap;
3. measure outputs beyond one beta-carotene projection so regulatory variation is
   not discarded by the objective;
4. choose genetic perturbations with demonstrable product-level effects;
5. preserve culture-level splits and strict lockbox controls;
6. test online reporter assimilation only after the generator or real culture
   has genuine hidden-state ambiguity that reaches future product;
7. calibrate uncertainty and distinguish irreducible process variation from
   model error;
8. validate strain construction, pathway stoichiometry, and experimental
   process constraints in wet lab.

These are not reasons to reinterpret the completed computational result. They
define the boundary between computational structural validation and an actual
experimental digital twin.

### Appendix A. Core equations

Regulatory dynamics:

\[
r_{t+1}=F_{\mathrm{rxncon}}(r_t,u_t).
\]

Smoothed interface channel:

\[
c_{t+1}=c_t+\alpha(c_t^\star(r_t,e_t)-c_t).
\]

Dynamic biomass and product:

\[
X_{t+1}=X_t+\Delta t\,v_{g,t}X_t,
\]

\[
B_{t+1}=\max(0,B_t+\Delta t[v_{B,t}X_t-k_{B,t}B_t]).
\]

Learned state-space:

\[
z_0=g_\theta(e,g),
\qquad
z_{t+1}=z_t+\Delta t f_\theta(z_t,e,g).
\]

Entropy rank:

\[
D_{\mathrm{entropy}}=\exp(-\sum_i p_i\ln p_i).
\]

Participation rank:

\[
D_{\mathrm{PR}}=(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2/\sum_i\lambda_i^2.
\]

### Appendix B. Dataset independence rule

The independent unit is a complete culture trajectory. A culture may contain 49
timepoints and 48 dynamic intervals, but it contributes one biological replicate
to train/validation/test allocation. Subsampling experiments select culture IDs,
not timepoint rows. Preprocessing, PCA bases, and normalization scales are fit
on training cultures only.

### Appendix E. Final interpretation

The project did not demonstrate that a neural state-space model universally
outperforms direct ML, that biosensors automatically improve product prediction,
or that real yeast can be accelerated by the tested workflow. It did establish a
progressively more defensible synthetic framework in which:

- a published high-dimensional regulatory model is separated from a compact
  learner;
- exact Yeast9/pFBA is used as hidden metabolic ground truth;
- observability and leakage are explicitly audited;
- culture counts and biological stages are separated from computation;
- direct baselines and negative controls are retained;
- and a bounded prospective virtual-search advantage is tested without treating
  physical-style simulated cultures as wet-lab evidence.

The remaining major step is replacing synthetic ground truth with sufficiently
rich, replicated real yeast data while preserving this observable-only,
mechanistically constrained, and leakage-controlled workflow.
